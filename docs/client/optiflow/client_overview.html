Discovery: Client Problem & Operational Context

Centre For Sight (CFS) operates a distributed nationwide retail network of optical stores across PAN India. Their entire physical inventory replenishment is supplied by a central logistics warehouse located in Dwarka, New Delhi.

Client Operational Baseline

The Dwarka warehouse serves as the central hub supplying 95+ retail store locations across three tiers:

  • 22 Grade A Stores: High velocity metropolitan flagships with weekly distribution cycles.
  • 30 Grade B Stores: Regional centers running on fortnightly replenishment schedules.
  • 43 Grade C Stores: Satellite clinics on monthly allocation cycles.

The Core Challenge

The logistical challenge is accurately calculating specific Planogram deficits at each store and determining exactly which frames or sunglasses (or acceptable substitutes) must be dispatched from Dwarka to maintain display compliance.

Because store managers cannot afford bare shelves, any replenishment delay directly degrades optical sales revenue.

Current Process: Manual Spreadsheet Distribution

The Legacy Problem

Currently, inventory replenishment is driven entirely by manual Excel spreadsheets. Merchandisers must export raw ERP dumps, execute complex nested VLOOKUPs to compare store stock on hand against target planograms, and manually guess which substitute frame to send when central warehouse stockouts occur.

This manual process introduces severe operational bottlenecks and subjective decision-making into the supply chain.

Proposed Process: Automated OptiFlow Replenishment

OptiFlow transitions Centre For Sight from manual spreadsheet analysis to an automated, intelligent allocation workflow.

Automates 95% of Standard Replenishment

We are deploying a deterministic Logic Engine paired with a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Dashboard. This eliminates manual Excel math entirely.

The merchandiser simply uploads the raw store and warehouse CSV/ERP data. OptiFlow automatically calculates Planogram deficits and executes allocations in under 3 seconds.

Intelligent Exception Handling

If an exact frame is out of stock in the Dwarka warehouse, the system automatically scores and finds the closest matching substitute (using heuristics: MRP ±20%, Color, Material, Shape).

The human manager only has to click "Approve" on rare exceptions, reducing a 5.5-hour manual ordeal into seconds.

09

Deterministic Allocation Logic Flow & Rules Engine

At the heart of OptiFlow is the Deterministic Allocation Engine (backend/app/business/allocation_engine.py). It replaces subjective human spreadsheets with strict mathematical constraints and a tiered waterfall matching algorithm.

The Deficit Formula: Deficit = Target (Facing + Depth) - Store Stock On Hand (SOH). If Deficit > 0, the allocation waterfall begins.

Tier 1: Recent Sales Match

Fetches exact SKUs sold in the store over the lookback window (Last 7 Days). Verifies warehouse stock and allocates directly.

Tier 2: Historical Sales Match

If Tier 1 stock is unavailable, searches exact SKUs sold over the last 6 months to maintain customer brand affinity.

Tier 3: Substitute Cascade

If exact SKUs are exhausted, evaluates substitute frames within MRP ±20%, systematically relaxing Color → Material → Shape.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we need technical skills to use OptiFlow?

No. If your team can export a spreadsheet and click approve, they can use OptiFlow.

What happens if an exact frame is out of stock?

OptiFlow suggests the closest available alternative and your manager approves it , no guesswork.

How long does a replenishment run take?

Seconds per store, versus 5.5 hours manually , a 99.8% time saving.

Can we see the history of past runs?

Yes. Every run is saved with a full, auditable history of what was planned and approved.

What data do you need from us to start?

Just your routine exports: current store stock, warehouse stock, your store list with grades, and display targets per store. Messy names and legacy codes are fine, OptiFlow maps them to clean master records automatically.

How does the pilot work?

We start with a small set of stores using your real data and run OptiFlow side by side with your current manual process, so you can compare the plans, timing, and accuracy before committing to a full rollout.

Who approves substitutions when stock runs short?

Your manager always has the final say. The engine suggests the closest available alternative in tiers, exact match first, then similar, then substitute, and nothing ships without your approval.

Do high-priority stores get preferential treatment?

Yes. Store grades (A, B, C) and dispatch cycles are built into the allocation rules, so flagship stores are served first while every location still gets a fair, transparent share.

Is our sales and inventory data safe?

Yes. Access is role-based, logins are secured, and every change, uploads, edits, approvals, is recorded in an immutable audit trail showing who did what and when.

What training does our team need?

Very little. If your team can export a spreadsheet and click approve, they can use OptiFlow. We run a short onboarding session and stay on hand through the pilot.

What happens when something looks wrong in a plan?

Exceptions are flagged in a review queue instead of slipping through. Your manager can adjust or override any line with a reason, and the override is logged, the system learns nothing silently, you stay in control.
